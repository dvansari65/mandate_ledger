# x402

How the ledger reads an x402 purchase: the cart a client buys, the
payment it signs, and the evidence that the payment settled. A client
needs one thing beyond the x402 specification, the
[binding nonce](#the-binding-nonce); everything else is x402 as written.

An agent's x402 payment, with the ledger's four calls in indigo. The agent
asks before it signs (2) and records what it signed (4); the merchant
records settlement (8) before it delivers (9). Any of the four can refuse,
and the purchase stops there.

x402 is pinned at **version 2** of the specification, as published at
<https://github.com/coinbase/x402> (`specs/x402-specification-v2.md`,
`specs/schemes/exact/scheme_exact_evm.md`). Networks are CAIP-2 names;
amounts are atomic units; the scheme is `exact`.

## The cart

An x402 purchase starts with a `402 Payment Required` whose
`PAYMENT-REQUIRED` header carries a `PaymentRequired` object: the
`resource` being sold and the `accepts` list of ways to pay. The client
picks one and echoes it as `accepted` in its `PaymentPayload`. The ledger's
cart is the payment payload without its signed `payload`: the resource,
the accepted requirement, and the extensions the client echoes.

```json
{
  "x402Version": 2,
  "resource": { "url": "https://api.example.com/premium-data", "description": "Access to premium market data", "mimeType": "application/json" },
  "accepted": {
    "scheme": "exact",
    "network": "eip155:84532",
    "amount": "10000",
    "asset": "0x036CbD53842c5426634e7929541eC2318f3dCF7e",
    "payTo": "0x209693Bc6afc0C5328bA36FaF03C514EF312287C",
    "maxTimeoutSeconds": 60,
    "extra": { "name": "USDC", "version": "2" }
  }
}
```

A client builds it from the 402 it received, with nothing added — and
never with the signed `payload`, which is refused: the signature would be
hashed into the cart, a re-signed retry would be a new purchase, and the
binding nonce derived from the cart could never be inside it.
`X402CartAdapter` turns the document into the engine's cart:

| Claim | From | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| merchant | `accepted.network` and `accepted.payTo` | a CAIP-10 account id, `eip155:84532:0x2096…`, address in lower case. The same address on another chain is another merchant, so a mandate names the chain it pays on |
| total | `accepted.amount` through the asset registry | atomic units become money in the token's symbol, with the token's decimals as the scale: `"10000"` of USDC is `0.010000 USDC` |
| category, line count | — | not carried by x402, so not claimed; a mandate that constrains them refuses the cart as `UNVERIFIABLE_SCOPE` |
| attestation | — | agent-reported: x402 does not sign its requirements, so a mandate that requires `merchant_signed` refuses every x402 cart as `ATTESTATION_INSUFFICIENT` |
| hash | the whole document | its RFC 8785 canonical form, unknown fields inside `resource` and `accepted` included, so the engine binds what was presented |

RFC 8785 writes numbers through IEEE-754 doubles. Every field the ledger
reads is a string or a small integer, so no claim is affected, but two
documents that differ only in the far digits of some other number are the
same cart.

What is refused, before the engine sees it:

- any `x402Version` but 2; any scheme but `exact`; a top-level field other
  than `x402Version`, `resource`, `accepted` and `extensions`;
- a network that is not an `eip155` chain written canonically
  (`eip155:8453`, never `base` or `eip155:08453`) — unsupported;
- an `asset` or `payTo` that is not an EVM address: unsupported when it is
  another kind of x402 value (a fiat code, a role constant such as
  `merchant`), malformed when it is a broken address — including a
  mixed-case one whose EIP-55 checksum is wrong, which is a typo;
- an asset the registry does not know on that chain — a token is never
  priced by guesswork — and a `payTo` that is the zero address or the
  token contract, where the payment would be lost;
- an amount that is not a whole decimal number written without leading
  zeros, or beyond what the adapter prices; a `maxTimeoutSeconds` that is
  not a positive integer;
- `extra.assetTransferMethod` other than `eip3009`, or `extra.paymentFlow`
  other than `authorization` (absent or `null` is the default); a missing
  `extra.name` or `extra.version`, or ones that are not the asset's own
  EIP-712 domain;
- a `resource` outside the reference schema's bounds, and only those: an
  empty `url`, a service name or tag that is not 1–32 printable ASCII
  characters, more than five tags, an `iconUrl` over 2048 characters.

## The asset registry

The wire names a token by contract address. Its symbol, its decimals and
the EIP-712 domain its contract signs under are not on the wire, and the
adapter does not guess them: it prices only tokens its `AssetRegistry`
knows. A registry is one deployment's choice of networks, and mainnet and
testnet come separately, as the reference implementation lists them in
`typescript/packages/mechanisms/evm/src/defaultAssets.ts`:

| Registry | Chain | USDC contract | EIP-712 domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| `AssetRegistry::base()` | Base, `eip155:8453` | `0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913` | "USD Coin", version 2 |
| `AssetRegistry::base_sepolia()` | Base Sepolia, `eip155:84532` | `0x036CbD53842c5426634e7929541eC2318f3dCF7e` | "USDC", version 2 |

Never load a testnet token in production. A mandate budgets money by
symbol, and USDC on a testnet is not USDC: a registry holding both would
let test carts spend a real budget. Add other tokens with `with_asset`,
which refuses a contract already registered on a chain and a symbol
another token on that chain already has: within one chain the money a
mandate budgets names one token, or a payment in one could pass for a
cart priced in the other.

## The binding nonce

x402 signs the payee, the amount, the token and the chain — the EIP-3009
authorization — and nothing about what is being bought: `accepted` and
`resource` ride beside the signature, unsigned, and anyone holding a
signed authorization can wrap it in any of them. So the ledger asks the
payer to sign which purchase the payment is for, in the one free field
EIP-3009 has: the nonce.

```text
nonce = keccak256(UTF-8("mandate-ledger:x402-nonce:v1:" + ctx))
```

`ctx` is the context id `authorize` returned, such as
`ctx_9b16b8b344ac38c22944eed7e8b3345e`. In TypeScript with viem,
`keccak256(toBytes("mandate-ledger:x402-nonce:v1:" + ctx))`; from a shell,
`cast keccak "mandate-ledger:x402-nonce:v1:$ctx"`. The client signs the
authorization with this nonce instead of a random one, and is otherwise
unchanged. It stays unique, as EIP-3009 needs: a context is one purchase,
and is paid once.

A payment whose nonce is the context's binding nonce binds to that
context. Any other payment binds to nothing and is refused as
`UNBOUND_PROOF`, including a genuine payment for the same cart under
another request key.

## The payment

`X402Rail` verifies an x402 v2 `PaymentPayload` — `exact` scheme, EIP-3009
transfer method — offline, with no call to a chain or a facilitator.

It refuses, as `PROOF_INVALID`, a payment no facilitator would settle,
with the reference facilitator's own error codes in the detail:

- a signature that does not recover to `authorization.from` under the
  token's EIP-712 domain on the chain `accepted` names — 65 bytes, `v` of
  27 or 28, `s` in the lower half of the curve order, exactly the rules
  of USDC's `ECRecover`. Contract-wallet signatures (EIP-1271, ERC-6492)
  need the chain to verify and are not accepted;
- `authorization.to` other than `accepted.payTo`, or `authorization.value`
  other than `accepted.amount`;
- `validBefore` less than six seconds away, or `validAfter` in the
  future — the reference facilitator's window, read from the ledger's
  clock;
- anything the cart adapter would refuse in `accepted`.

Everything else it reports and the engine decides:

| The engine compares | With | Refusal |
|---|---|---|
| `authorization.value`, as money in the token's symbol | the authorized amount | `AMOUNT_MISMATCH` |
| the [binding nonce](#the-binding-nonce) | the context being paid | `UNBOUND_PROOF` |
| `authorization.to` on the payment's chain, as a CAIP-10 account id | the authorized merchant | `MERCHANT_BINDING_MISMATCH` |
| the authorization's identity | every authorization already spent | `NONCE_ALREADY_USED` |

The payment's single-use nonce is the authorization as the token
contract keys it — the payer's nonce on one token on one chain,
`eip155:84532:<token>:<payer>:<nonce>` — because the chain executes a
nonce once, whatever else was signed with it. Its reference adds when it
expires, `…:<nonce>:<validBefore>`, which settlement needs.

The freshness checks are for a payment being recorded. A retry of the
payment a context already recorded is answered as that payment whenever
it comes, expired or not, with its signature verified again; a different
payment is checked in full.

## Settlement

The rail judges evidence the host fetched; it makes no network calls
itself. Two kinds, set by a `FinalityPolicy`:

- **A receipt** — `eth_getTransactionReceipt` for the settlement
  transaction, with the chain head it was read at and the network it came
  from. It is final when it succeeded, it carries the token's
  `AuthorizationUsed(payer, nonce)` for this authorization and a
  `Transfer(payer, merchant, amount)` matching what was authorized, and it
  has `confirmations` blocks, its own included. Fewer is pending. A
  reverted receipt is pending too: a failed settlement spends nothing, and
  the authorization can still execute until it expires. A receipt that
  does not show this payment is refused as `FINALITY_INVALID`.
- **The facilitator's answer** — x402's `SettlementResponse` to
  `POST /settle`. It does not name the authorization it settled, only the
  network, the payer and optionally the amount, which the rail checks.
  With `trust_facilitator`, its success is final. Its failure is final
  only once the authorization can no longer execute: a failed settlement
  spends nothing, and whoever holds the signed authorization can still
  settle it until `validBefore`, so until then — and `expiry_grace` after,
  the operator's bound on how far the chain's block time can trail this
  host's clock — the reservation stands and the answer is pending.
  `settlement_pending`, the specification's one non-terminal code, is
  always pending. Without `trust_facilitator`, every answer is pending
  until a receipt arrives.

Not yet: concluding failure without a trusted facilitator. That needs
chain state read after `validBefore` — the authorization's
`authorizationState` — and comes with the service's finality webhook.
Until then, a paid context the facilitator is not trusted to fail stays
pending.
